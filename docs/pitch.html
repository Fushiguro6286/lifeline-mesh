Ten minutes before you walk on

Set the stage

  • Phone A (iQOO 15), role "I need help": How it works (speed icon) → On-device AI says Ready · Gemma 4 E4B on GPU. Screen timeout 10 minutes, brightness up, Do Not Disturb off.
  • Real phones B and C, if the three-phone relay has been rehearsed: B volunteer in the middle, C "Ambulance". Otherwise run the whole demo in Simulation mode on A and say so; the red "Simulated" banner says it too.
  • Mobile data and Wi-Fi internet off, Bluetooth and location on. Near a window for 5 minutes so GPS has a fix.
  • Screen mirroring to the projector tested. Close the remote-control app and anything that can pop up.
  • Simulation switched on at least 2 minutes early, so shelters, alerts and teams already exist.
The 10 minutes

Script

0:00The problem · 40 s
Say

"When a flood or a landslide hits, the first thing to go is the network."

"Towers lose power, lines get cut, the one network everybody depends on goes silent at the exact moment people need it. Someone trapped on a roof can't call, can't send a location, can't even know if anyone heard them. And the rescue teams arriving have no idea who needs help first, or where."

"We asked one question: what if the phones in that neighbourhood could carry the message themselves?"

0:40The idea · 40 s
Say

"This is Lifeline Mesh. Panic in, clear alert out, even with no network."

"Three promises. One: it works with no SIM, no tower and no internet; phones pass alerts to each other over Bluetooth, like a relay race. Two: the AI runs on the phone itself, here a Gemma model on the iQOO 15's GPU, so it works offline too. Three: every number you see is measured on the phone, and anything simulated is labelled Simulated."

DoHold up phone A. Point at the status bar: no mobile data, no Wi-Fi internet.
1:20Demo: panic in · 2 min 40 s
Say

"Imagine I'm on a roof with my family. I don't fill in a form; I just speak, in Hindi, Kannada or English."

Do
  1. SOS tab → Speak: "Water is rising, four of us are trapped on the roof, my father is sick." → Continue.
  2. Point at the review sheet: Trapped, Medical, 4 people, Urgent, "Understood by: On-device AI", and the packet size (about 60 bytes).
  3. Send alert. Point at the status card as it moves: Passed to 2 nearby phones → Reached a rescue team's phone → Help is coming. The phone vibrates.
Say

"The AI turned panic into a structured alert: what is wrong, how many people, how urgent, and the GPS position. The whole alert is about 60 bytes, smaller than one line of this slide."

"It hopped phone to phone. And look: before any person answered, my phone told me 'your alert reached an ambulance team's phone, two hops away'. Then the team said help is coming, and my phone vibrated, even on silent."

"If I just type 'please help us', the app won't send something useless: it asks one calm question, in my language, to get what's missing. And if I can't speak at all, I hold this button for 3 seconds."

Do (if time)Hold Hold for SOS for 3 seconds: an SOS goes out with a strong vibration.
If it failsSpeech fails in the noise: tap Type, same sentence. The alert doesn't reach C in 20 s: Settings → Simulation mode on, send again, and say "this is the simulated neighbourhood".
4:00Demo: the rescuer · 2 min
Say

"Now the other side. I'm an ambulance team, and alerts are arriving from all over the neighbourhood."

Do
  1. Rescue tab (role Ambulance). Point at the situation report: three lines written on the phone, every number checked against the alerts.
  2. Who goes where: the alerts split between teams, the right team for each need. On the one suggested for you, Take it.
  3. Your plan: the job, the minutes along the roads, and "then take them to School hall".
  4. Map tab: the team lines along real roads, the green drop-off leg to the shelter, the minute labels.
  5. Ask the mesh: let a judge ask, e.g. "Who needs help first?"
Say

"Every team phone runs the same rules on the same data, so two ambulances never go to the same person while someone else waits. No control room, no server."

"The routes are on an offline map of Bengaluru, built into the app. The AI can explain things, but it is never allowed to invent a number: if its answer has a number that isn't in the alerts, the phone shows the computed answer instead."

If it failsThe model is busy or slow: the card shows the computed text, which is correct. Say "the AI is optional; the rules always work".
6:00Demo: when things go wrong · 1 min 30 s
Say

"Disasters don't stay still. Watch what happens when things go wrong."

Do (simulation on)
  1. Mesh tab → Block a road ahead → Map tab: the team's route goes around the fallen tree.
  2. Mesh tab → Ravi's battery to 8 % → banner Show: "Ravi's phone is your only link to 5 phones · battery 8 %".
  3. Mesh tab → Fill the nearest shelter → SOS tab, Where to go: it switches to the Temple hall.
  4. SOS tab → Find someone → type a name, e.g. "Meena".
Say

"A road gets blocked: the team re-plans around it. A relay phone runs low: it goes into survival mode and passes on only life-saving messages, and the app tells rescuers exactly where to place another phone so nobody is cut off. A shelter fills up: everyone is sent to the next one. And a family can type a name and see 'Meena checked in safe, 2 hops away'."

7:30Under the hood · 1 min 15 s
Say

"How it works, in four sentences."

"One: each alert is a tiny binary packet, a 19-byte header and the essentials, with GPS stored to a tenth of a metre. Two: phones flood it outward over Google Nearby Connections, each one passing it on once, at most six phones far, keeping it for six hours if nobody is in range. Three: on-device AI picks the best model this phone can run (Gemma 4 E4B here, a smaller model on a smaller phone, rules only on an old phone) and every AI output is checked before it's shown. Four: when any phone finds internet, it syncs to a server and brings back official warnings; nothing depends on it."

DoHow it works screen (speed icon): the last alert step by step, the model card, Benchmarks.
8:45Measured, not claimed · 45 s
Say

"We were strict about honesty. These numbers were measured on this iQOO 15, and the app shows 'Not measured' where we don't have one."

"The model's first word comes in under a second, about 21 tokens a second on the GPU, and it passed our tests in English, Hindi and Kannada. With the model removed, every feature still works on rules. The core logic has over 300 automated tests."

DoBenchmarks screen; point at GPU vs CPU and at "Not measured" where it appears.
9:30Close · 30 s
Say

"Every phone in a flooded street already has the radio to save a life. Lifeline Mesh turns them into one network that keeps working when everything else stops: it understands panic on the phone, gets the alert through phone by phone, and helps rescuers decide who to reach first."

"Panic in, clear alert out, even with no network. Thank you."

After the pitch

Questions judges are likely to ask

What if the other phones don't have the app?

Messages hop only through phones with the app. Two answers: anyone can share the app offline from phone to phone, and the phone can put its SOS in its Bluetooth name, so any phone nearby that opens its Bluetooth list sees it.

How far does it reach?

Each hop is Bluetooth range, tens of metres, more in the open. An alert can cross six phones, and a phone that moves (a volunteer walking) carries stored alerts to the next group. Real outdoor range: we will measure and show it.

Why on-device AI and not the cloud?

There is no cloud in a disaster. The model runs on the phone's GPU, offline. The app picks a model per phone, and with no model it still works on rules.

Can the AI make a dangerous mistake?

It never decides alone. It suggests fields the person checks before sending, numbers are always computed in code and checked, and first aid only shows vetted guides word for word.

What about battery?

Below 20 % a phone goes into survival mode: fewer hellos, only life-saving messages, AI paused. The phone records drain per hour in each mode.

Privacy: can anyone see where I am?

Only rescue teams see people's positions. Others see teams. IDs are random per install, never a phone number. Each link is encrypted by Nearby Connections.

What stops spam or fake alerts?

Each phone accepts at most 10 messages a minute from one sender, duplicates are dropped, and messages expire. Team roles are self-declared today; verification is a next step.

Did the NPU work?

Not yet: it needs a model built for this exact chip. The GPU is the accelerated path today, and the Benchmarks screen says so honestly.
