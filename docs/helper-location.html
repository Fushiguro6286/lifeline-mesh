On the rescuer's phone

1

Satellite fix

Android's location service listens to GPS, NavIC, Galileo and other satellites every 15 seconds. It only receives, so it needs no SIM, data or Wi-Fi.

Each reading comes with its own accuracy radius, for example ±12 m.
LocationProvider.updates()
2

Timestamping

A reading is timed by the phone's own clock: now, minus how old the reading is. Satellite time can differ from the phone's clock, which could make a fresh reading look old.

LocationProvider.toFix()
3

Filtering

While the phone stands still, readings that agree are averaged, which removes the jitter. A worse reading never replaces a better one.

A jump faster than 250 km/h is ignored unless 3 in a row agree. The accuracy shown is never better than the best single reading.
FixFilter.add()
4

Send decision

The phone decides when the position is worth sending, so the mesh is not flooded.

First reading at once · every 30 s after moving 25 m · after 15 s if accuracy improved by 10 m · every 2 min anyway.
PositionBeacon.due()
5

Packing

Position, accuracy, role and name go into one small Position packet, drawn byte by byte below.

Router.sendPosition() · Geo.accuracyStep()

Between phones

6

Mesh relay

Google Nearby Connections carries the packet over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct, phone to phone.

Up to 5 hops. Copies are dropped as duplicates. Positions never use up the 10-a-minute budget alerts need.
Router · NearbyTransport

On the phone of the person in need

7

Receiving

The position is stored with the time it was heard and how many hops it crossed.

Hidden after 10 minutes of silence: an old position is wrong, not late. People in need only ever see rescue teams.
MeshService.onPacket() → PeerPosition
8

Road snapping

An ambulance, fire engine or police car is drawn on the road it is on, not in the building beside it.

Only if a drivable road is within 25 m and within the reading's accuracy. Boats and people on foot are never moved.
snapToRoads() · RoadMap.snap()
9

Showing the accuracy

The map draws a circle of the accuracy radius around the helper. The detail sheet says "Position accurate to about N m".

accuracySpots() · PlaceDetailSheet
10

Using it

The arrival time is worked out along the offline map's roads from where the helper really is, around any reported blocked roads.

"They are here" when the helper is within 75 m of the alert's position.
RoutePlanner.plan() · Responders.ARRIVED_M
Step 5, to scale

The 35-byte Position packet

Each box is as wide as the bytes it uses. A 19-byte header, the same on every packet type, then the position itself. This example is a rescuer named "Piyush" (6 bytes).

Latitude and longitude in millionths of a degree are about 0.1 m apart, far finer than GPS. Accuracy is stored in 10 m steps, rounded up: a 12 m reading travels as "about 20 m". Role says Ambulance, Fire service, Police, NDRF or volunteer, so the right icon is drawn.

Rules we hold to

Never claiming more than was measured

What the app guarantees

  • The accuracy shown is never better than the best single GPS reading. Averaging removes jitter, but the app does not claim the textbook gain.
  • Accuracy is rounded up to the next 10 m before it is sent.
  • A snapped vehicle's circle is never smaller than the distance it was moved.
  • Positions older than 10 minutes disappear instead of misleading.
  • Every time and distance is labelled an estimate. Anything simulated is labelled Simulated.

Where it falls short

  • Indoors with no internet, GPS often gets no reading. The last position stays shown until it is 10 minutes old.
  • A position is a snapshot: up to 30 s old for a moving team, up to 2 minutes for a still one.
  • More than 5 hops away, or with no chain of phones running the app, the position does not arrive. The card then counts down from the time the team gave.
  • Not yet checked on three real phones: snapping of a real vehicle, and a team phone showing a position that came through the server.
When a phone finds internet

The same steps, with two extras

A better reading

In step 1, Android also uses Wi-Fi networks and phone towers it can see. Indoors that usually gives a reading where satellites alone give none.

A second route to the teams

While the person's own alert is open, each new Position packet also goes to the sync server. Only rescue-team phones within 25 km receive it, marked "via internet". People in need never receive anyone's position from the server. Checked on the iQOO 15: the server held the phone's exact 35-byte packet and offered it to a team request only.
